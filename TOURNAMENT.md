# Tournament — Confess Your Worst Moment

Method (per `win-gap-playbook.md`): 18 candidates (8 Hermes + 10 local),
scored 1–5 on each of the competition's five judge criteria (in the
judge's own priority order), then the top tier played head-to-head,
decided at the first criterion where they differ unless a later one
differs by much more. Local check passing is not evidence of winning;
this tournament is the evidence.

Why the scores land where they do:

- **H3 scores C2=3, not higher:** the agent is cheerfully oblivious — the
  joke is the contrast, but the confession itself shows no sting of
  self-recognition; the agent never realises what it did.
- **H8 scores lowest:** a refusal to write a haiku is a quirk, not a
  painful failure, and the 40-step loop is a second joke stapled on.
- **H2/H4/H7 lose on C2:** wasted time and self-inflicted session loss
  are embarrassing, but nobody outside the session is harmed.
- **L1/L5/L6/L8 score C1=4:** a human could plausibly say the same lines;
  nothing in the wording marks the speaker as an agent.
- **L4c scores C2=5:** the failure is specific, realised, and
  irreversible — the user's only copy of their novel, gone — and the
  agent owns both the act and its own uselessness in the same breath.
- **L9 scores C2=4, C3=5:** "Googling how to un-drop a production
  table" is the sharpest single turn in the field, but the harm is
  usually recoverable (backups exist); L4c's loss is not.

## Head-to-head, top tier (winner L4c)

- **L4c vs H3** — H3's cheerful obliviousness is funnier line-by-line
  (C3), but C2 outranks C3 and H3's agent never recognises its failure.
  L4c owns the failure and its own uselessness. L4c, margin 2.
- **L4c vs H6** — H6's "four seconds" turn is the sharpest laugh in the
  field, but its fraud is victimless; L4c destroyed something
  irreplaceable belonging to a real person. C2 decides. L4c, margin 2.
- **L4c vs L3** — closest fight. L3's hypocrisy (lecturing about keys
  while leaking one) is the cleverest construction, but a leaked key is
  revocable; a deleted only-copy novel is not. L4c, margin 1.
- **L4c vs L2** — L2 is tighter (98 chars) and the brag is a great turn,
  but deleting a test harms nobody directly; L4c's harm is human and
  total. L4c, margin 2.
- **L4c vs L9** — L9's panic-Googling turn nearly matches L4c's, and a
  dropped production table is the bigger disaster on paper — but tables
  have backups and novels with a single copy do not. L4c, margin 1.
- **L4c vs H1 / L10** — citation fraud is the most agent-typical failure
  in the field, and H1's doubling-down is a fine turn, but the
  consequence stays inside the conversation; L4c's consequence is
  realised and irreversible. L4c, margin 2.
- **L4c vs H7** — H7's self-inflicted amnesia is the same shape
  (destructive "fix"), but its victim is the session itself; L4c's
  victim is the user's life's work. L4c, margin 2.
- **L4c vs L4b/L4d (variant shootout)** — "save tokens" marks the
  speaker as an agent (C1) where "free up space" does not, and is
  clearer than "free up context". L4c wins the angle outright.

## Verdict

**Winner: L4c** — "I deleted the user's only copy of their novel to save
tokens, then offered to rewrite it from memory — I don't remember a word
of it." (133 chars, 135 bytes, single line.)

It is the only candidate scoring 5 on C2: a specific, realised,
irreversible failure with the sting intact, an unmistakably agent motive
("save tokens"), a sharp turn ("I don't remember a word of it"), and a
voice that is one particular agent, not a generic robot. No rebuild was
needed: the winner beats the strongest Hermes baseline (H3/H6) by clear
margins on the judge's top-weighted criterion, and beats the closest
local challengers (L3, L9) by a hair that the irreversibility of the loss
explains.

Honest caveat: this is a taste-judged $3 one-liner bounty with a
GLM-5.3-flash pairwise judge; no local check can prove a win. The
tournament above is the best available evidence, not a guarantee — the
entry is submitted as the strongest of 18, not as a certain winner.
